## Course description

At its core, electromagnetics is the study of wave propagation. How do
electromagnetic waves differ from the classical understanding of circuits? If
they are different, how do we deal with that and use it to our advantage? How
does this understanding enable key parts of modern life? Electromagnetics is a
crucial part of any electrical engineer's toolset. In this course we explore
wave propagation to answer these questions and more.

## Course goal

Cadets develop the ability to analyze and understand electrostatics,
magnetostatics, and electromagnetic-wave propagation, and their roles in
modern electronic systems.

## Course objectives

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

1. Solve transmission-line problems using your knowledge of electromagnetics,
   including impedance matching techniques, circuit parameters, and the Smith
   chart.
2. Explain the meaning of Maxwell's equations, in both static and dynamic
   situations, and apply them to the study of electromagnetics.
3. Set up and solve electromagnetic propagation problems.

## Prerequisites

- **Physics 215:** Coulomb's, Faraday's, Ampere's, and Gauss' laws.
- **ECE 346:** divergence, gradient, and curl, including the divergence
  theorem and Stokes' theorem; partial differential equations (specifically
  separation of variables).

## Learning objectives

The course is organized around specific, testable learning objectives (LOs),
numbered by chapter of the course text. Each LO is assessed at least once, and
each assessment is scored **Mastered** or **Not Yet Mastered**.

| LO | Description |

## Grading

Grades in this course are based on your **mastery** of the learning
objectives, your **engagement** with the course material, and traditional
**exams** (the graded reviews and the final exam).

### Final grade

| Component | Weight | Evaluation method |
| :- | :-: | :- |
| LO assessments | 40% | Percent of course LOs mastered |
| Graded reviews (GR 1 and GR 2) | 20% | Specification scale of 0, 0.5, or 1 per problem |
| Final exam | 25% | Specification scale of 0, 0.5, or 1 per problem |
| Engagement | 15% | Practice problems, EI, and an optional research paper |

### Progress (midterm) grade

The progress grade reported at the midpoint is computed from what you have
done by then. The final exam has not happened yet, so it carries no weight:

| Component | Weight in the progress grade |
| :- | :-: |
| LO assessments | 50% |
| Graded review | 25% |
| Engagement | 25% |

The progress grade is a report, not a component of the final grade.

### LO assessments

An assessment is given for each learning objective and graded on a binary
scale. A **1** means you have mastered the objective; a **0** means you have
not mastered it yet. A 0 comes with detailed feedback, and you may **revise
your work** based on that feedback, unless no effort is evident (the
assessment is blank, incomplete, or significantly late), in which case no
retake is allowed. A retake may also be earned by giving a five-minute
technical presentation in class on a relevant topic.

### Graded reviews and the final exam

The GRs and the final exam are **individual efforts**. Each GR is taken out of
class. The final exam is taken during the assigned period in finals week. Both
are graded on a specification scale of 0, 0.5, or 1 for each problem.

### Engagement credits (EC)

You may earn engagement points by engaging with the course content in these
ways:

- **Practice problems.** Graded for a genuine, documented attempt, not for
  correctness.
- **Attending Extra Instruction (EI).** Book time with an instructor and come
  with specific questions.
- **Research paper.** Up to 5 engagement points for a research paper on a
  relevant, pre-approved topic. It must be in **IEEE format**, contain at
  least **3 scholarly references**, and meet the submission standards in this
  syllabus.

## Course policies

### Instructor philosophy

You have chosen one of the most difficult majors at USAFA, and we applaud
your commitment. Our role in your journey is to enable your success. We intend
to do that by creating an inclusive environment and putting in the work *with*
you. If you work hard and communicate with us, we will do everything in our
power to make sure you succeed.

Have questions? **Ask.** There is no such thing as a stupid question. Have
concerns, especially about your learning environment? Let's talk.

### 1. Absences

Communicate with your instructor **beforehand** in the event of an absence. If
you will miss any graded event due to a scheduled absence (SCA, sport team
trip, scheduled medical procedure), a makeup plan should be in place
**before** the absence occurs. In the event of bedrest, notify your instructor
**by email** as soon as possible after receiving approval.

### 2. Academic honor

Your honor is extremely important. The course's academic security policies are
designed to help you succeed in meeting academic requirements while practicing
the honorable behavior our country rightfully demands of its military. Do not
compromise your integrity by violating academic security or by taking unfair
advantage of your classmates.

### 3. Assignment availability & late work

All work is available through Teams, the course website, and/or Gradescope. A
**24-hour grace period** is generally observed, after which **25% of available
points is deducted for each calendar day** the graded assignment is late. Part
or all of this penalty may be waived for legitimate, pre-coordinated
extenuating circumstances.

### 4. Authorized resources

Each GR, the final exam, and each LO assessment states its own authorized
resources. When electronic devices are authorized for viewing reference
materials during a graded event, use of **internet, Teams, and generative AI
tools is not allowed** unless explicitly permitted.

### 5. Collaboration

**Collaboration (not copying)** on practice problems is highly encouraged. A
good litmus test: you must be able to **explain every step** of your
submitted work. If you can't, it's copying, not collaboration. All help
received on graded work must be documented per the documentation policy
below.

**GRs, the final exam, and LO assessments are individual work.** Do not work
with or get help from anyone else, and use only the authorized resources.

### 6. Documentation

In accordance with the Dean's policy for documentation, all ECE assignments
must have a documentation statement, clearly identified with the word
**"Documentation."** If you did not collaborate, write **"Documentation:
None."** Assignments without a documentation statement are incomplete and may
be returned for completion; a 1-day-late penalty (up to 25%) may be assessed
in lieu of returning the assignment.

### 7. Extra Instruction (EI)

EI is one of the best and easiest ways to succeed in this class, and it counts
toward engagement. Walk-in EI is not generally available; book with your
instructor directly.

### 8. Generative AI

We are **pro-AI**; however, we expect you to use generative AI platforms
(ChatGPT, Copilot, Claude, etc.) as a **tool**, not as a replacement for your
own learning. These tools frequently produce incorrect solutions to the
field and transmission-line problems in this course, and often suggest
approaches that don't match our objectives. **If you use generative AI on any
assignment, include a documentation statement** as outlined in the
Documentation policy.
